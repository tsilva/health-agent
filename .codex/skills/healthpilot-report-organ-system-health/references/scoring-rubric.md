# Organ and System Scoring Rubric

This is a transparent, nonvalidated ordinal rubric. Use it consistently; do not imply clinical validation or probability.

## Five dimensions

Assign each dimension `0`, `0.5`, `1`, `1.5`, or `2`.

### Structural integrity

- `0`: acute failure, catastrophic damage, or end-stage structural disease
- `0.5`: severe or advanced structural abnormality
- `1`: unknown, mixed, old/resolved abnormality, or mild abnormality of unclear significance
- `1.5`: mostly reassuring structure with minor or incomplete findings
- `2`: recent, direct, organ-appropriate evidence of normal structure

### Physiologic function

- `0`: organ failure or immediately dangerous loss of function
- `0.5`: severe functional impairment
- `1`: unknown, mixed, or mild functional concern
- `1.5`: broadly preserved function with minor limitation or incomplete testing
- `2`: recent, direct evidence of strong normal function

### Symptoms and real-world functional impact

- `0`: severe disabling symptoms or acute red-flag presentation attributable to the system
- `0.5`: substantial persistent symptoms or functional loss
- `1`: unknown, nonspecific, intermittent, or mild symptoms
- `1.5`: minimal symptoms with preserved daily function and some direct support
- `2`: explicit, sustained absence of relevant symptoms plus strong demonstrated function

Do not assign `2` solely because no symptom was mentioned.

### Disease burden and control

- `0`: critical, uncontrolled, or end-stage disease
- `0.5`: severe active or poorly controlled disease
- `1`: unknown status, unresolved differential, or controlled disease with meaningful residual burden
- `1.5`: minor, well-controlled, or low-impact disease/risk with objective support
- `2`: strong current evidence of no material disease burden

Treatment success can support control, but treatment presence does not erase underlying burden.

### Trajectory, reserve, and resilience

- `0`: rapidly worsening or recurrent decompensation
- `0.5`: clear deterioration or poor recovery/reserve
- `1`: unknown trajectory, stable mixed state, or inadequate longitudinal data
- `1.5`: stable or improving with reasonable recovery and longitudinal support
- `2`: durable favorable trend, strong reserve, and consistent recovery evidence

## Total score interpretation

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 0–1.5 | Critical or near-failure; urgent direct evidence should exist |
| 2–3.5 | Severe or advanced dysfunction |
| 4–4.5 | Moderate confirmed impairment or major unresolved concern |
| 5–5.5 | Indeterminate/mixed, or mild-to-moderate concern |
| 6–6.5 | Mild concern or broadly functional with important limitations |
| 7–7.5 | Generally healthy/functioning with minor risk or incomplete direct evidence |
| 8–8.5 | Strongly evidenced good health with only small issues |
| 9–9.5 | Excellent, recent, multimodal, longitudinally reassuring evidence |
| 10 | Exceptional directly evidenced health across all five dimensions; use rarely |

A `5` can mean evidence-neutral uncertainty. It is not a statement that the organ is 50% healthy.

## Confidence and plausible range

- `High`: recent direct organ-specific structure/function evidence plus consistent longitudinal or symptom evidence
- `Moderate`: at least one direct source or repeated concordant indirect evidence, with manageable gaps
- `Low`: sparse, stale, indirect, ambiguous, or conflicting evidence

Use a plausible range that includes the point score:

- High confidence: usually ±0.5 to 1
- Moderate confidence: usually ±1 to 2
- Low confidence: usually ±2 to 3

Clamp ranges to 0–10. These are sensitivity ranges, not statistical confidence intervals.

## Guardrails

- Assign `5.0`, Low confidence, and a wide range when all five dimensions are unassessed.
- Do not assign above `7.0` based only on absence of documented problems.
- Require direct recent evidence in multiple dimensions for `9.0` or higher.
- Cap an actively failing system at `2.0` even if some historical tests were normal.
- Let definitive recovery evidence raise a previously low score; do not permanently penalize resolved disease.
- Keep future genetic susceptibility and family history mainly in the risk/trajectory discussion unless current phenotype exists.
- Explain every adjustment that moves a dimension away from the neutral `1.0` anchor.
